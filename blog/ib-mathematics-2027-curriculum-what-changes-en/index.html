IB Mathematics 2027 curriculum: what students should check before planning revision

A practical guide to the IB Mathematics curriculum transition from 2027: how to check the correct AA or AI guide, assessment session and revision materials.

IB Mathematics revision is entering a transition period. The International Baccalaureate states that new Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches and Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation courses begin teaching in 2027, with first assessment in 2029. That does not mean every current student should replace their revision plan. It means the first task is to identify the course guide and assessment session that actually apply to you.

Direct answer. Before choosing resources, confirm your Mathematics course (AA or AI), level (SL or HL), first year of study and examination session. Use the guide issued for your cohort and your school's instructions; then build revision around the assessment you will sit, rather than around a generic list of IB topics.

Why the cohort matters

Two students can both say “I take IB Maths AA” while working from different course documents. A student who begins the new course in 2027 will follow a different guide from a student completing the current course in 2027 or 2028. A revision video, worksheet or past-paper recommendation only helps when it matches the student's actual programme.

The official IB Mathematics overview is the right place to verify the transition dates and find the relevant subject brief. Your school remains the source for the guide, sequence and assessment advice that apply to your cohort.

The four details to write at the top of your revision plan

Before sorting topics, record:

  1. 1.Course: Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA) or Applications and Interpretation (AI).
  2. 2.Level: Standard Level (SL) or Higher Level (HL).
  3. 3.Cohort and session: when you started the course and when you will be assessed.
  4. 4.School requirements: current unit, internal deadlines, approved technology and any teacher guidance.

These facts prevent the most expensive revision mistake: preparing a question format, topic list or assessment expectation that belongs to another cohort.

AA and AI still need different study habits

The names are similar, but the useful study routine is not identical. AA students should give sustained attention to algebraic structure, functions, calculus and communicating a mathematical argument. AI students should repeatedly practise choosing a model, using technology thoughtfully, interpreting output in context and evaluating whether a result makes sense.

Both courses require more than a final answer. In either route, write enough working, diagrams, graphs and interpretation for another reader to follow the decision. This makes a calculator result checkable and exposes a weak assumption before it costs marks.

How to audit resources during a curriculum change

For every resource, ask five questions:

  • Does it name the correct course: AA or AI?
  • Is it intended for SL, HL or both?
  • Does it state the syllabus or assessment years it covers?
  • Does the question style match your guide and teacher's materials?
  • Does it make the method and interpretation visible, not just the final answer?

If the first three answers are unclear, do not discard the resource automatically. Use it for underlying mathematics only after checking that the specific question type is relevant to your course. Keep current school material and officially released guidance at the centre of the plan.

A revision cycle that survives the transition

Start with a question from your own course. Classify the first point of failure as one of four types: missing knowledge, an incorrect method choice, an execution error or weak communication. Then use a short resource route to repair that point:

  1. 1.rebuild the concept with one clear example;
  2. 2.solve a changed version without notes;
  3. 3.attempt an exam-style question from the correct course;
  4. 4.review the written evidence, timing and interpretation; and
  5. 5.return to the error a few days later.

The cycle is stable even when a syllabus changes because it trains transferable mathematical decisions rather than memorising a one-off worksheet.
