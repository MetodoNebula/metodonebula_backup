Enzyme questions in GCSE Biology test more than a definition. You may need to explain a graph, plan a required practical, identify variables and use particle-level reasoning without turning the answer into a list of facts.

> **Direct answer.** Enzymes are biological catalysts with an active site. Temperature and pH affect the rate because they change collisions and, at extreme values, can alter the active site's shape.

## What an enzyme does

An enzyme has an active site with a shape that allows a specific substrate to bind. The enzyme-substrate complex forms, a reaction happens, and the products leave. The enzyme itself can be used again.

The useful exam language is specific: say that the active site's shape is complementary to the substrate. Do not say an enzyme works on every substance.

## Temperature: explain the whole graph

At low temperatures, particles have less kinetic energy, so successful collisions happen less often. As temperature rises, the rate increases until the optimum.

Above the optimum, bonds in the enzyme can be disrupted. The active site changes shape, the substrate no longer fits as effectively and the enzyme is denatured. A high-temperature fall in rate is not explained simply by saying that particles slow down.

## pH and the active site

Each enzyme has an optimum pH. Very acidic or alkaline conditions can disturb bonds that maintain the active site's shape. In a graph, a low rate at both extremes does not mean pH has stopped all collisions; it means the enzyme is no longer in its most effective structure.

## Required practical: planning a fair test

A common practical investigates how pH affects amylase breaking down starch. Iodine solution turns blue-black if starch is present. The endpoint is when the iodine no longer turns blue-black.

A clear method changes one independent variable, such as pH, and controls the others:

| Variable type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Independent | pH of the buffer |
| Dependent | Time until starch is no longer detected |
| Control | Temperature, volumes, amylase concentration and mixing method |

Use water baths to keep temperature constant. Repeat each pH value and calculate a mean to reduce the effect of random variation.

## Converting time into rate

If equal amounts are used, a simple relative rate can be calculated as:

$$
\text{rate}=\frac{1}{\text{time}}.
$$

This matters because a smaller time means a larger rate. Plotting time directly can make a graph look inverted; always label axes and explain which quantity you have graphed.

## Evaluation points that earn marks

- Use a range of pH values with equal intervals around the expected optimum.
- Repeat measurements and identify anomalies using evidence, not preference.
- Use apparatus with an appropriate resolution for volume and time.
- Keep temperature stable with a water bath.
- Avoid claiming that repeats remove all error; they improve reliability.

## Common GCSE Biology mistakes

- Writing that the enzyme is used up in the reaction.
- Confusing denaturation with the enzyme being killed.
- Naming a control variable without saying how it is controlled.
- Treating rate and time as the same quantity.
- Explaining the optimum only with «more collisions» and ignoring active-site shape.
