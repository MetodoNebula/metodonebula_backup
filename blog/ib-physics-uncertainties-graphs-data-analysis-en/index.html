IB Physics uncertainties and graphs: data analysis with worked examples

IB Physics data analysis explained through uncertainty, percentage uncertainty, gradients, error bars and original worked examples.

In IB Physics, a measured value without its uncertainty is incomplete data. The aim is not to make an experiment look exact; it is to show what the apparatus can genuinely support and whether the conclusion survives that limitation.

Direct answer. Record a measurement with a sensible absolute uncertainty, propagate that uncertainty through the calculation, then use error bars and the gradient to judge how strongly the data support a model. More decimal places do not create more accuracy.

Absolute, fractional and percentage uncertainty

An absolute uncertainty has the same unit as the measurement. A length reported as 12.4±0.1 cm12.4\pm0.1\ \mathrm{cm}12.4±0.1 cm has an absolute uncertainty of 0.1 cm0.1\ \mathrm{cm}0.1 cm. Its fractional uncertainty is 0.1/12.40.1/12.40.1/12.4, and its percentage uncertainty is

0.112.4×100≈0.8%.\frac{0.1}{12.4}\times100\approx0.8\%.12.40.1​×100≈0.8%.

Percentage uncertainty lets unlike measurements be compared fairly. An uncertainty of ±0.1 cm\pm0.1\ \mathrm{cm}±0.1 cm is significant for a 1.0 cm length but much less important for a 1.0 m length.

Propagating uncertainty through a calculation

For multiplication, division and powers, percentage uncertainties are combined at school level. If ρ=m/V\rho=m/Vρ=m/V, and mass has a 2%2\%2% uncertainty while volume has a 3%3\%3% uncertainty, the density has an estimated percentage uncertainty of

2%+3%=5%.2\%+3\%=5\%.2%+3%=5%.

For addition or subtraction, absolute uncertainties are combined instead. If two independently measured lengths are added as 4.2±0.1 cm4.2\pm0.1\ \mathrm{cm}4.2±0.1 cm and 7.8±0.1 cm7.8\pm0.1\ \mathrm{cm}7.8±0.1 cm, report 12.0±0.2 cm12.0\pm0.2\ \mathrm{cm}12.0±0.2 cm before converting that result into a percentage if needed.

The rule matters because it tells you where experimental quality is being lost. Reducing a tiny mass uncertainty does little if volume dominates the final uncertainty.

The volume measurement contributes more uncertainty, so improving the balance would not be the first priority.

Graphs are models, not decoration

Put the independent variable on the horizontal axis and the dependent variable on the vertical axis. Label both with quantity and unit, choose a scale that uses most of the plotting area and plot uncertainty bars where they are meaningful.

For a linear relationship y=mx+cy=mx+cy=mx+c, the gradient mmm and intercept ccc often have physical meaning. If a force–extension graph follows F=kxF=kxF=kx, the gradient is the spring constant kkk. It is not enough to calculate a gradient: state its unit and what it represents in the experiment.

Gradient uncertainty from limiting lines

First draw a best-fit line that represents the overall trend. Then draw the steepest and shallowest reasonable lines that still pass through the uncertainty bars. If their gradients are mmax⁡m_{\max}mmax​ and mmin⁡m_{\min}mmin​, estimate

Δm=mmax⁡−mmin⁡2.\Delta m=\frac{m_{\max}-m_{\min}}{2}.Δm=2mmax​−mmin​​.

Then Δm=(5.2−4.4)/2=0.4 N m−1\Delta m=(5.2-4.4)/2=0.4\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}Δm=(5.2−4.4)/2=0.4 Nm−1, so a defensible result is k=(4.8±0.4) N m−1k=(4.8\pm0.4)\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}k=(4.8±0.4) Nm−1.

What the graph can and cannot claim

If the uncertainty bars overlap a proposed line, the data may be consistent with that model; they do not prove it. A non-zero intercept may indicate a systematic offset, but it may also be consistent with zero within uncertainty. Separate the observation from the explanation: first describe the pattern, then give a physical reason that fits it.

A concise data-analysis check

  • Report raw measurements to the resolution of the instrument, not to invented precision.
  • Carry extra digits in intermediate steps, then round the final uncertainty sensibly.
  • Give units for every plotted axis, gradient and derived constant.
  • Do not force a line through the origin unless the model requires it and the data support it.
  • Use uncertainty to qualify a conclusion rather than hiding a disagreement.
