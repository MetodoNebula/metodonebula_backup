IB Mathematics AI probability: binomial and normal distributions

Probability-distribution questions test whether a model fits a situation before they test calculator commands. A binomial calculation can be numerically flawless and still be invalid if the trials are not independent or the probability is not constant.

Direct answer. Define the random variable, justify the model from the context, translate the requested probability into symbols and then use technology or a formula with the correct inclusive endpoints. Finally, decide whether the numerical answer is plausible for the distribution.

When a binomial model is appropriate

Write X∼Bin(n,p)X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)X∼Bin(n,p) only when there is a fixed number of trials, two outcomes per trial, a constant probability of success and independent trials. Here nnn is the number of trials and ppp is the probability of the defined success.

If 12 independently manufactured components each have a 0.08 probability of being defective, then the number of defective components can be modelled as X∼Bin(12,0.08)X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(12,0.08)X∼Bin(12,0.08). The word independently matters: sampling without replacement from a small known batch would not automatically meet the condition.

Exact, cumulative and complementary probability

For exactly rrr successes,

P(X=r)=(nr)pr(1−p)n−r.P(X=r)=\binom nrp^r(1-p)^{n-r}.P(X=r)=(rn​)pr(1−p)n−r.

For at most rrr, use P(X≤r)P(X\le r)P(X≤r). For at least rrr, a complementary calculation is often clearer:

P(X≥r)=1−P(X≤r−1).P(X\ge r)=1-P(X\le r-1).P(X≥r)=1−P(X≤r−1).

The r−1r-1r−1 is essential. “At least 3” includes 3, so the complement is 0, 1 or 2, not 0 through 3.

Worked binomial example

With X∼Bin(12,0.08)X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(12,0.08)X∼Bin(12,0.08), find the probability of at least two defective components. Use the complement:

P(X≥2)=1−P(X=0)−P(X=1).P(X\ge2)=1-P(X=0)-P(X=1).P(X≥2)=1−P(X=0)−P(X=1).
=1−(0.92)12−(121)(0.08)(0.92)11≈0.249.=1-(0.92)^{12}-\binom{12}{1}(0.08)(0.92)^{11}\approx0.249.=1−(0.92)12−(112​)(0.08)(0.92)11≈0.249.

The expected number is np=0.96np=0.96np=0.96, so a probability near one quarter for two or more defects is more credible than a result close to one. That expectation is not the answer, but it is a useful sense check.

The normal model and standardization

A continuous normal random variable is written X∼N(μ,σ2)X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)X∼N(μ,σ2), where μ\muμ is the mean and σ\sigmaσ is the standard deviation. Standardization converts a value into a number of standard deviations from the mean:

z=x−μσ.z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}.z=σx−μ​.

If test times follow T∼N(54,62)T\sim N(54,6^2)T∼N(54,62) minutes, then 66 minutes corresponds to z=(66−54)/6=2z=(66-54)/6=2z=(66−54)/6=2. A probability above 66 minutes is the area to the right of z=2z=2z=2, which technology can calculate.

Reading calculator output accurately

Technology may use commands for a lower bound, upper bound, mean and standard deviation. For P(48<T<60)P(48<T<60)P(48<T<60), the lower and upper bounds are 48 and 60. For P(T>60)P(T>60)P(T>60), use 60 as the lower bound and an appropriately large upper bound or a complementary probability, depending on the calculator.

Because normal distributions are continuous, P(T<60)P(T<60)P(T<60) and P(T≤60)P(T\le60)P(T≤60) are equal. That is unlike a discrete binomial distribution, where inclusive wording changes which integer outcomes are included.

Model criticism belongs in the answer

A normal model can assign a small probability to an impossible negative value if the mean is not far from zero relative to the standard deviation. That does not necessarily invalidate every estimate, but it is a limitation to acknowledge. Similarly, a bell-shaped histogram alone is not proof of normality; context, sample size and outliers matter.

A short checklist for distribution questions

  • Define success and the random variable before selecting a distribution.
  • Translate “at least”, “at most” and “between” into inequalities.
  • Check binomial assumptions instead of only typing nnn and ppp.
  • Use σ\sigmaσ, not σ2\sigma^2σ2, as the denominator in a zzz-score.
  • Give a probability as a decimal or percentage with sensible rounding and an interpretation in context.
