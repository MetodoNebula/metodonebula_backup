GCSE Physics: reflection and refraction without mixing up frequency, speed and wavelength

Master reflection and refraction in GCSE Physics: normals, angles, speed, frequency, wavelength and exam explanations.

A wave does not simply “turn” at a boundary. In reflection it remains in the original medium; in refraction it enters another medium and changes speed. The diagram only becomes reliable after the normal has been drawn and every angle is measured from it.

Direct answer. A refracted wave keeps the same frequency because the source fixes it. Its speed and wavelength may change through v=fλv=f\lambdav=fλ. If the wave slows down, it bends towards the normal; if it speeds up, it bends away.

The normal organises the diagram

The normal is perpendicular to the boundary at the point of incidence. Incidence, reflection and refraction angles are measured between each ray and the normal, never between a ray and the surface.

For reflection:

i=rreflection.i=r_{\text{reflection}}.i=rreflection​.

This remains true for a curved mirror: construct the local normal first.

What changes during refraction

The source continues oscillating at the same rate:

f1=f2.f_1=f_2.f1​=f2​.

Since v=fλv=f\lambdav=fλ, changing speed changes wavelength. If v2<v1v_2<v_1v2​<v1​, then λ2<λ1\lambda_2<\lambda_1λ2​<λ1​: the wavefronts become closer.

For two media:

sin⁡isin⁡r=v1v2.\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}.sinrsini​=v2​v1​​.

You do not need a memorised direction rule if you compare the speeds.

Worked example

A wave moves at 12 m s−112\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}12 ms−1 in the first medium and 8 m s−18\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}8 ms−1 in the second. Its frequency is 4 Hz4\ \mathrm{Hz}4 Hz.

λ1=124=3 m,λ2=84=2 m.\lambda_1=\frac{12}{4}=3\ \mathrm{m}, \qquad \lambda_2=\frac{8}{4}=2\ \mathrm{m}.λ1​=412​=3 m,λ2​=48​=2 m.

The frequency stays at 444 Hz and the wavelength decreases. The refracted ray bends towards the normal.

Water, light and sound

The physical argument is shared, but “denser medium” is not a universal shortcut. Light usually travels more slowly in glass than in air, while sound can travel faster in solids than in gases. Compare the relevant wave speeds.

A complete explanation

A three-mark response should connect:

  1. 1.The speed changes at the boundary.
  2. 2.The frequency remains constant.
  3. 3.The wavelength changes because v=fλv=f\lambdav=fλ, producing the direction change.

Critical angle and total internal reflection

When light tries to move from a medium in which it travels more slowly into one in which it travels faster, the refracted angle increases. At one particular incidence angle, called the critical angle, the refracted ray runs along the boundary: r=90∘r=90^\circr=90∘. Above that incidence angle, no transmitted ray remains and total internal reflection occurs.

Both conditions matter: light must be travelling towards the faster medium and its incidence angle must exceed the critical angle. In an optical fibre, repeated total internal reflection keeps the signal inside the core. Writing only that “light bounces” does not explain the role of the boundary and the angle.

Reading a transparent-block experiment

Trace the block, draw a thin incident ray and mark two points on each straight ray section. Remove the block and join the points with a ruler. Construct each normal and place the protractor centre exactly at the incidence point before measuring. Repeating this for several angles allows sin⁡i\sin isini to be compared with sin⁡r\sin rsinr.

Thick pencil lines, a tilted normal or measuring from the block face create systematic error. In an experimental-evaluation question, suggest thin lines, repeated readings and a wide range of angles rather than simply saying “be more careful”.

Mistakes that lose marks

  • Measuring angles from the surface.
  • Changing frequency at the boundary.
  • Confusing reflection with refraction.
  • Treating amplitude and frequency as the same quantity.
  • Drawing the refracted ray without comparing speeds.

Final exam check

Check the normal, units, direction and v=fλv=f\lambdav=fλ. If the wave slows down but your wavelength increases, the answer is inconsistent.
