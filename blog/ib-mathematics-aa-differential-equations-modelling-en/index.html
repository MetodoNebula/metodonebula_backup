IB Mathematics AA: differential equations and modelling

IB Mathematics AA differential equations explained with separation of variables, initial conditions, exponential models and interpretation.

A differential equation describes a relationship involving a changing quantity and its rate of change. Solving it means finding a function that satisfies both the equation and, when given, the initial condition.

Direct answer. For a separable equation, put all terms involving the dependent variable on one side and all terms involving the independent variable on the other, integrate, then use the initial condition before making numerical predictions.

Exponential change

If a quantity PPP changes at a rate proportional to itself,

dPdt=kP.\frac{dP}{dt}=kP.dtdP​=kP.

Separating variables gives

dPP=k dt.\frac{dP}{P}=k\,dt.PdP​=kdt.

After integration,

ln⁡∣P∣=kt+C,\ln|P|=kt+C,ln∣P∣=kt+C,

so P=AektP=Ae^{kt}P=Aekt. An initial value P(0)=P0P(0)=P_0P(0)=P0​ gives A=P0A=P_0A=P0​.

If k>0k>0k>0 the model grows; if k<0k<0k<0 it decays. The sign is a conclusion from the equation, not a label added afterwards.

A model with a limiting value

Not every growth process remains exponential. If a quantity approaches a carrying level LLL, a model may include

dPdt=kP(1−PL).\frac{dP}{dt}=kP\left(1-\frac{P}{L}\right).dtdP​=kP(1−LP​).

The signs tell the story: when 0<P<L0<P<L0<P<L, the rate is positive; when P>LP>LP>L, the rate is negative. Before solving, use the differential equation itself to identify equilibrium values and whether they are stable.

A separable example

Suppose

dydx=3xy.\frac{dy}{dx}=3xy.dxdy​=3xy.

Then

dyy=3x dx.\frac{dy}{y}=3x\,dx.ydy​=3xdx.

Integrating gives ln⁡∣y∣=32x2+C\ln|y|=\frac32x^2+Cln∣y∣=23​x2+C, hence

y=Ae3x2/2.y=Ae^{3x^2/2}.y=Ae3x2/2.

Modelling limits

Every differential-equation model makes assumptions. A population model with unlimited exponential growth eventually becomes unrealistic; a decay model assumes a constant proportional rate. State the interval and physical conditions where the model is intended to apply.

Common mistakes

  • Integrating 1/y1/y1/y as 1/y21/y^21/y2 instead of ln⁡∣y∣\ln|y|ln∣y∣.
  • Applying an initial condition before obtaining the general solution.
  • Dropping the integration constant.
  • Giving a formula without interpreting units or the sign of the rate.

Course alignment
