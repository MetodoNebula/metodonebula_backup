The IB Physics formula booklet is useful only when each symbol is connected to a physical model. A formula does not decide whether a force is external, whether a velocity has a sign, or whether energy is conserved in the system you chose.

Direct answer. Before substituting values, define the system, choose a positive direction and write every quantity in SI units. Then use the formula whose assumptions match the situation and check the final unit and sign.

Forces and acceleration

Newton's second law is

∑F=ma.\sum F=ma.∑F=ma.

The sum is a vector sum. If right is positive, a 12 N driving force and 5 N resistive force give ∑F=12−5=7\sum F=12-5=7∑F=12−5=7 N. For a 2 kg object, a=7/2=3.5 m s−2a=7/2=3.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}a=7/2=3.5 ms−2.

The equation does not say that every force equals mamama. It says the resultant force does. Draw the free-body diagram first whenever several forces act.

Work, energy and power

For a constant force parallel to displacement,

W=Fs.W=Fs.W=Fs.

More generally, W=Fscos⁡θW=Fs\cos\thetaW=Fscosθ. The angle is between the force and the displacement, not automatically the angle drawn in the diagram.

Kinetic and gravitational potential energy are

Ek=12mv2,ΔEp=mgΔh.E_k=\frac12mv^2,\qquad \Delta E_p=mg\Delta h.Ek​=21​mv2,ΔEp​=mgΔh.

If a 0.50 kg object rises 3.0 m, its gravitational potential energy changes by approximately 0.50×9.81×3.0=14.70.50\times9.81\times3.0=14.70.50×9.81×3.0=14.7 J. This says nothing yet about its final speed; you must account for every relevant transfer.

Momentum and impulse

Momentum is

p=mv,p=mv,p=mv,

and impulse is the change in momentum:

FΔt=Δp.F\Delta t=\Delta p.FΔt=Δp.

For collisions in an isolated system, total momentum is conserved. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions, so do not assume it without justification.

Worked problem: force from motion data

A 0.80 kg0.80\ \mathrm{kg}0.80 kg trolley moves east at 2.5 m s−12.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}2.5 ms−1. A constant westward resultant force acts for 0.40 s0.40\ \mathrm{s}0.40 s and leaves it moving east at 1.5 m s−11.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}1.5 ms−1.

Take east as positive. The momentum change is

Δp=m(v−u)=0.80(1.5−2.5)=−0.80 kg m s−1.\Delta p=m(v-u)=0.80(1.5-2.5)=-0.80\ \mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}.Δp=m(v−u)=0.80(1.5−2.5)=−0.80 kgms−1.

Therefore F=Δp/Δt=−2.0 NF=\Delta p/\Delta t=-2.0\ \mathrm{N}F=Δp/Δt=−2.0 N. The negative sign agrees with the stated westward force. A magnitude alone would lose the vector information supplied by the calculation.

Choosing between energy and kinematics

Use kinematics when time, displacement, velocity and constant acceleration are connected. Use energy when the path is not important but work and changes in energy are. A falling object with air resistance is not an automatic conservation-of-mechanical-energy question: resistance transfers energy to the surroundings.

Force-time graphs: area has a meaning

The area under a force-time graph is impulse, so it gives the change in momentum. A constant 6.0 N6.0\ \mathrm{N}6.0 N force acting for 0.30 s0.30\ \mathrm{s}0.30 s has an impulse of 1.8 N s1.8\ \mathrm{N\,s}1.8 Ns. For a triangular pulse, use 12×base×height\tfrac12\times\text{base}\times\text{height}21​×base×height rather than the rectangle rule. In either case, attach a direction: an impulse can reduce a positive momentum or reverse it.

This is also a useful consistency check. If a graph gives a positive impulse but the calculated final velocity is smaller on a positive axis, one of the signs has been reversed.

Formula-booklet checks

  • Convert kilometres per hour to metres per second before using mechanics equations.
  • Keep velocity signs in one dimension; a negative result may be physically correct.
  • Use newtons, joules, kilograms and seconds consistently.
  • State the system before claiming conservation of energy or momentum.
  • Quote a sensible number of significant figures from the data supplied.
