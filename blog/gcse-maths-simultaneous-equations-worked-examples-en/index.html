A pair of simultaneous equations is not asking you to solve two unrelated equations. It asks for values that make both equations true at the same time. That idea connects elimination, substitution, graphs and contextual problems: each method finds a common intersection.

Direct answer. Use elimination when coefficients can be matched easily, substitution when one variable is already isolated and a graph when the intersection must be interpreted. Always test the solution in both original equations.

Question 1: direct elimination

Solve:

2x+y=11,qquad3x−y=9.2x+y=11,qquad 3x-y=9.2x+y=11,qquad3x−y=9.

The coefficients of yyy are opposites. Add the equations:

5x=20⇒x=4.5x=20\Rightarrow x=4.5x=20⇒x=4.

Substitute into the first equation:

2(4)+y=11⇒y=3.2(4)+y=11\Rightarrow y=3.2(4)+y=11⇒y=3.

Check in the second equation: 3(4)−3=93(4)-3=93(4)−3=9. The solution is (4,3)(4,3)(4,3).

Question 2: matching coefficients

Solve:

3x+2y=16,qquad5x−3y=7.3x+2y=16,qquad 5x-3y=7.3x+2y=16,qquad5x−3y=7.

The lowest common multiple of 2 and 3 is 6. Multiply the first equation by 3 and the second by 2:

9x+6y=48,qquad10x−6y=14.9x+6y=48,qquad 10x-6y=14.9x+6y=48,qquad10x−6y=14.

Adding gives:

19x=62⇒x=6219.19x=62\Rightarrow x=\frac{62}{19}.19x=62⇒x=1962​.

Substitute without rounding:

3(6219)+2y=16⇒2y=11819⇒y=5919.3\left(\frac{62}{19}\right)+2y=16 \Rightarrow 2y=\frac{118}{19} \Rightarrow y=\frac{59}{19}.3(1962​)+2y=16⇒2y=19118​⇒y=1959​.

Fractions do not mean that the method failed. Early rounding would make the final check less accurate.

Question 3: substitution

Solve:

y=2x+1,qquad4x+y=16.y=2x+1,qquad 4x+y=16.y=2x+1,qquad4x+y=16.

The first equation already gives yyy. Substitute it into the second:

4x+(2x+1)=16,4x+(2x+1)=16,4x+(2x+1)=16,
6x=15⇒x=2.5.6x=15\Rightarrow x=2.5.6x=15⇒x=2.5.

Then:

y=2(2.5)+1=6.y=2(2.5)+1=6.y=2(2.5)+1=6.

The solution (2.5,6)(2.5,6)(2.5,6) satisfies 4(2.5)+6=164(2.5)+6=164(2.5)+6=16.

Question 4: ticket problem

An event sells 30 tickets. Adult tickets cost €8 and junior tickets cost €5. The total income is €201. How many of each ticket were sold?

Let aaa be adult tickets and jjj be junior tickets:

a+j=30,qquad8a+5j=201.a+j=30,qquad 8a+5j=201.a+j=30,qquad8a+5j=201.

Multiply the first equation by 5:

5a+5j=150.5a+5j=150.5a+5j=150.

Subtract this from the income equation:

3a=51⇒a=17.3a=51\Rightarrow a=17.3a=51⇒a=17.

Therefore, j=30−17=13j=30-17=13j=30−17=13. The contextual check is €17(8)+13(5)=20117(8)+13(5)=20117(8)+13(5)=201. State both quantities in the final answer.

Question 5: linear-quadratic system

Find the intersection points of:

y=x+2,qquady=x2−4.y=x+2,qquad y=x^2-4.y=x+2,qquady=x2−4.

Both expressions equal yyy, so equate them:

x+2=x2−4,x+2=x^2-4,x+2=x2−4,
x2−x−6=0,x^2-x-6=0,x2−x−6=0,
(x−3)(x+2)=0.(x-3)(x+2)=0.(x−3)(x+2)=0.

Thus, x=3x=3x=3 or x=−2x=-2x=−2. Find each yyy-coordinate from the line:

x=3⇒y=5,qquadx=−2⇒y=0.x=3\Rightarrow y=5,qquad x=-2\Rightarrow y=0.x=3⇒y=5,qquadx=−2⇒y=0.

The two intersections are (3,5)(3,5)(3,5) and (−2,0)(-2,0)(−2,0). A linear-quadratic system may have two, one or no real solutions.

Choosing the method

  • An isolated variable: substitution.
  • Equal or opposite coefficients: elimination.
  • Visual interpretation or an estimate: graph.
  • A line and a curve: equate expressions and solve the resulting equation.

Errors that lose marks

  • Multiplying only one term in an equation.
  • Adding equations without controlling signs.
  • Finding xxx but not yyy.
  • Failing to define variables in context.
  • Rounding a fraction too early.
  • Checking only one equation.
