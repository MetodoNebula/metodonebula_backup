On a heating curve, a horizontal line does not mean that energy transfer has stopped. The supplied energy is changing particle arrangement rather than increasing temperature.

Direct answer. Use Q=mcΔTQ=mc\Delta TQ=mcΔT when temperature changes within one state and Q=mLQ=mLQ=mL during a change of state. On a plateau, temperature remains constant while internal energy increases.

Temperature and internal energy

Temperature is linked to the particles' mean kinetic energy. Internal energy includes kinetic energy and potential energy associated with particle interactions.

Heating a solid usually increases particle vibration. During melting, energy partly overcomes the forces holding the structure together.

Sloping sections

When state does not change:

Q=mcΔT.Q=mc\Delta T.Q=mcΔT.

mmm is mass, ccc is specific heat capacity and ΔT\Delta TΔT is temperature change.

Plateaus

During melting or boiling:

Q=mL.Q=mL.Q=mL.

LLL is specific latent heat. Energy changes particle separation and potential energy rather than mean kinetic energy, so temperature remains constant.

Power and time

E=Pt.E=Pt.E=Pt.

Graph gradient

At constant power, a steeper line means temperature rises faster. For the same mass and power, a material with lower ccc gives a steeper gradient.

Plateau length depends on the energy needed for the state change and the input power.

Experiment to determine ccc

Measure the block mass, place a heater and thermometer in good thermal contact and record temperature while the electrical power is known. For heating time ttt:

c=PtmΔT.c=\frac{Pt}{m\Delta T}.c=mΔTPt​.

Insulation and a lid reduce energy loss. The block should be dry and the sensors should fit tightly because an air gap increases thermal resistance. Taking many readings allows transferred energy to be plotted against temperature; the gradient is mcmcmc.

The experimental value of ccc is often too high if all electrical energy is assigned to the block even though some escapes. A complete evaluation gives the direction of the effect rather than merely listing “heat loss”.

Common mistakes

  • Using Q=mcΔTQ=mc\Delta TQ=mcΔT on a plateau.
  • Claiming internal energy is constant because temperature is constant.
  • Confusing ccc and LLL.
  • Substituting mass in grams.
  • Assuming 100% efficiency when losses are stated.

Particle explanation

A complete answer names energy, motion, separation and forces. “Particles expand” is inaccurate: their average separation usually changes; the particles themselves do not grow.
