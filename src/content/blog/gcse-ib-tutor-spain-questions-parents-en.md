Finding GCSE or IB support in Spain can feel confusing because the label alone says very little. “Maths tutor” may mean a lesson that repeats classroom notes, while a useful plan identifies the exact course, assessment conditions and evidence of what is currently blocking the student. The same distinction matters whether the help is online or local.

> **Direct answer.** Choose support that starts from the student's actual programme, level, assessment date and recent work. Ask how the tutor will turn a marked question or mock into a specific next task, and how the student will practise independently between sessions.

## 1. Does the plan identify the exact course?

For GCSE or IGCSE, ask for the subject, tier where relevant and exam board. For IB Mathematics, ask whether the student is taking AA or AI and SL or HL. For IB Physics and Chemistry, record the course level, current unit and the form of assessment causing difficulty.

A tutor does not need to promise that every student follows the same route. The better sign is a willingness to check the specification, school materials and recent questions before proposing one.

## 2. What evidence will shape the first lesson?

A useful diagnostic can begin with one attempted question, a recent marked homework task or a mock paper. A score alone is not enough. The tutor should be able to separate a conceptual gap from a calculation slip, a timing problem or an unclear written explanation.

For example, an incorrect Physics answer may originate in the diagram, the model, the unit conversion or the calculation. Giving another formula sheet will not repair all four possibilities.

## 3. How will exam technique be taught?

Ask how the student will move from topic exercises to unfamiliar questions. Strong support includes timed work at the right stage, feedback against the available markscheme or rubric, and a method for revisiting errors later. It does not mean completing full papers every week without analysing why marks were lost.

For GCSE, the tier and question wording should guide practice. For IB, the written method, interpretation and use of technology need to match the course rather than being treated as optional extras.

## 4. Is there a study route between lessons?

Students often understand an example during a lesson but lose the process by the next week. Ask what happens after the call. A good answer names the next concept, a worked example, independent questions and a way to check the result.

The [Método Nebula Campus](/campus/gcse-ib/) is designed for that bridge: original theory, visible worked reasoning, graded practice and exam-style application. The purpose is not to fill every evening with worksheets; it is to make the next action clear.

## 5. Can the explanation work in English and Spanish?

For international-school students in Spain, language can be a real obstacle even when the academic idea is understood. Bilingual explanation can help unpack vocabulary or a difficult model. The final practice, however, should still use the language, notation and command terms required by the course.

Ask whether the tutor can distinguish between explaining a concept in a familiar language and training a response in the assessment language. They are related but not identical tasks.

## 6. Are the limits of support clear?

Good academic support develops the student's own reasoning. It can explain techniques, review a plan and give feedback on the student's work. It should not complete assessed coursework, write an Internal Assessment or promise a grade without evidence about the starting point and available study time.

Clear limits protect both the student and the value of the work they submit.

## 7. What should a family send before contacting a tutor?

Prepare a short message with:

- subject and programme (GCSE, IGCSE or IB);
- level, tier and exam board where known;
- next mock, coursework deadline or final-exam date;
- one recent question, marked task or mock result; and
- the aspect that currently feels hardest.

This replaces a vague request for “extra help” with enough information to discuss an appropriate route.
