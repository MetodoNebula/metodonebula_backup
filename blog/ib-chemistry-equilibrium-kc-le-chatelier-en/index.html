IB Chemistry: equilibrium, Kc and Le Châtelier explained

IB Chemistry equilibrium explained with dynamic equilibrium, Kc expressions, reaction quotient and Le Châtelier reasoning.

Chemical equilibrium is dynamic: forward and reverse reactions continue, but at equal rates. Concentrations remain constant in a closed system because the two changes balance, not because the reaction has stopped.

Direct answer. Write the balanced equation, build the equilibrium expression from concentrations of the relevant species and compare the reaction quotient with KcK_cKc​ when deciding the direction of change. A catalyst changes rates, not the equilibrium constant or position.

Constructing the equilibrium constant expression

For

N2+3H2⇌2NH3,\mathrm{N_2}+3\mathrm{H_2}\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{NH_3},N2​+3H2​⇌2NH3​,
Kc=[NH3]2[N2][H2]3.K_c=\frac{[\mathrm{NH_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{N_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]^3}.Kc​=[N2​][H2​]3[NH3​]2​.

The exponents come from the coefficients in the balanced equation. Pure solids and pure liquids have constant activity in the usual school-level treatment, so they are omitted from the expression.

Reaction quotient versus equilibrium constant

The expression for QcQ_cQc​ has the same form as KcK_cKc​, but uses current concentrations rather than equilibrium concentrations.

  • If Qc<KcQ_c<K_cQc​<Kc​, the reaction proceeds forward overall.
  • If Qc>KcQ_c>K_cQc​>Kc​, the reaction proceeds backward overall.
  • If Qc=KcQ_c=K_cQc​=Kc​, the system is at equilibrium.

This comparison is more precise than trying to memorise a direction for every possible disturbance.

Le Châtelier with a reason

Increasing the concentration of a reactant raises the denominator of QcQ_cQc​ and makes QcQ_cQc​ smaller than KcK_cKc​, so the system shifts forward. Temperature is different: it changes the value of KcK_cKc​ because it changes the balance of energies. Treat exothermic and endothermic directions as part of the equation.

Pressure affects gaseous equilibria when the number of gas moles differs between sides. It does not automatically favour a reaction merely because pressure changed.

For coefficients other than one, the changes must follow the stoichiometric ratio. That is why the balanced equation comes before the table.

Temperature deserves a separate argument

Changing concentration or pressure changes QcQ_cQc​ while KcK_cKc​ stays fixed at constant temperature. Changing temperature changes KcK_cKc​ itself. State whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic before predicting the direction.

Pressure is a particle-count argument

For a gaseous equilibrium, decreasing the volume makes all gas concentrations larger at once. The direction then depends on which side has fewer moles of gas. For N2+3H2⇌2NH3\mathrm{N_2}+3\mathrm{H_2}\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{NH_3}N2​+3H2​⇌2NH3​, four moles of gas on the left correspond to two on the right, so compression favours the right-hand side. If both sides have the same number of gaseous moles, pressure has no positional effect even though individual concentrations changed.

This reasoning applies only to gases. A solid added to a heterogeneous equilibrium does not enter the concentration expression in the usual school-level model.

Essential checks

  • Balance before writing KcK_cKc​.
  • Do not include catalyst concentration in the expression.
  • Distinguish a change in equilibrium position from a faster approach to equilibrium.
  • Use a table of initial, change and equilibrium amounts when algebra is needed.
