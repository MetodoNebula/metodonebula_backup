GCSE Maths: quadratic equations and completing the square step by step

Quadratic equations GCSE: factorising, the quadratic formula, completing the square, discriminant and exam-style checks.

A quadratic equation is not solved by choosing a favourite method. First bring every term to one side, recognise the structure, then choose the quickest reliable route. In GCSE questions, the marks often sit in that decision as much as in the arithmetic.

Direct answer. Write the equation as ax2+bx+c=0ax^2+bx+c=0ax2+bx+c=0. Factorise when the factors are visible, use the quadratic formula when they are not, and complete the square when the vertex or minimum value matters. Substitute your answers back at the end.

Factorising: the fast route

For

x2−5x+6=0,x^2-5x+6=0,x2−5x+6=0,

look for two numbers whose product is 666 and whose sum is −5-5−5. They are −2-2−2 and −3-3−3, so

(x−2)(x−3)=0.(x-2)(x-3)=0.(x−2)(x−3)=0.

The zero-product rule gives x=2x=2x=2 or x=3x=3x=3. Do not divide by a bracket: it could be zero, which is exactly what you are trying to find.

The quadratic formula

When factorising is not obvious, use

x=−b±b2−4ac2a.x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}.x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​.

For 2x2+x−4=02x^2+x-4=02x2+x−4=0, identify a=2a=2a=2, b=1b=1b=1 and c=−4c=-4c=−4 before substituting. Writing those three values first protects you from a common error: losing the sign of ccc.

Completing the square

Completing the square turns a quadratic into vertex form. For example,

x2+6x+5=(x+3)2−4.x^2+6x+5=(x+3)^2-4.x2+6x+5=(x+3)2−4.

This shows immediately that the graph has a minimum value of −4-4−4 at x=−3x=-3x=−3. It is useful for solving, sketching and explaining a maximum or minimum in context.

What the discriminant tells you

The expression b2−4acb^2-4acb2−4ac is the discriminant. It predicts the number of real roots:

  • positive: two real roots;
  • zero: one repeated real root;
  • negative: no real roots.

This is especially useful when a question asks whether a line meets a parabola, even before you calculate the coordinates.

A GCSE checking routine

  1. 1.Rearrange so one side is zero.
  2. 2.Choose the method from the structure, not from habit.
  3. 3.Keep brackets around negative substitutions in the formula.
  4. 4.Substitute each solution into the original equation.
