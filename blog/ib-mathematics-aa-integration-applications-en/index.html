IB Mathematics AA integration: areas, volumes and interpretation

IB Mathematics AA integration explained with definite integrals, signed area, volumes of revolution and fully worked original examples.

Integration in Mathematics AA is not just antidifferentiation. A definite integral accumulates signed change, which is why a correct integral can still be the wrong answer when the question asks for geometric area or a physically meaningful total.

Direct answer. Sketch or analyse the function first, identify where it crosses an axis, integrate on the correct intervals and interpret the sign. For a volume of revolution, write the radius explicitly before choosing the formula.

Indefinite and definite integrals do different jobs

An indefinite integral produces a family of functions:

∫3x2 dx=x3+C.\int 3x^2\,dx=x^3+C.∫3x2dx=x3+C.

The constant is required because differentiation removes constants. A definite integral produces a number:

∫abf(x) dx=F(b)−F(a),\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a),∫ab​f(x)dx=F(b)−F(a),

where F′(x)=f(x)F'(x)=f(x)F′(x)=f(x). Its sign records whether the graph lies mostly above or below the horizontal axis over that interval.

Area=−∫02(x−2) dx+∫25(x−2) dx.\text{Area}=-\int_0^2(x-2)\,dx+\int_2^5(x-2)\,dx.Area=−∫02​(x−2)dx+∫25​(x−2)dx.

Area between two curves

For y=xy=xy=x and y=x2y=x^2y=x2 between 0 and 1, x≥x2x\ge x^2x≥x2, so

Volumes of revolution: identify the radius

When the region under y=f(x)y=f(x)y=f(x) is rotated around the xxx-axis, a typical disk has radius f(x)f(x)f(x) and area π[f(x)]2\pi[f(x)]^2π[f(x)]2. Therefore

V=π∫ab[f(x)]2 dx.V=\pi\int_a^b[f(x)]^2\,dx.V=π∫ab​[f(x)]2dx.

V=π∫02x2 dx=π[x33]02=8π3.V=\pi\int_0^2x^2\,dx=\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2=\frac{8\pi}{3}.V=π∫02​x2dx=π[3x3​]02​=38π​.

The radius is a distance. If rotation is about y=3y=3y=3, the radius is not f(x)f(x)f(x) automatically: it is the vertical distance ∣3−f(x)∣|3-f(x)|∣3−f(x)∣. Write that distance before squaring it.

Integration in a physical context

If velocity is given by v(t)v(t)v(t), then ∫abv(t) dt\int_a^b v(t)\,dt∫ab​v(t)dt is displacement. Total distance requires splitting the calculation wherever velocity changes sign, just like a geometric area problem. Units provide a quick check: (m s−1)×s=m(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})\times\mathrm{s}=\mathrm{m}(ms−1)×s=m.

For a rate such as litres per minute, integrating over minutes gives litres. A bare numerical result is incomplete if it has not been interpreted in the context and correct units.

Common errors that a sketch prevents

  • Forgetting +C+C+C in an indefinite integral.
  • Treating signed area as total area when the graph crosses the axis.
  • Choosing bottom minus top between curves.
  • Squaring the wrong radius in a volume calculation.
  • Reporting an answer without units or a conclusion in context.
