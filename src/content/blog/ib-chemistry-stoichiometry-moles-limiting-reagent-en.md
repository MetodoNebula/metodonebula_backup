Stoichiometry is the link between a balanced equation and measurable amounts. The coefficients describe mole ratios; they do not describe masses or volumes until those quantities have been converted to moles.

> **Direct answer.** Balance the equation first, convert every known amount to moles, apply the coefficient ratio, then convert only the requested amount back to mass, volume, concentration or particle number.

## The mole as a bridge

Mass and amount of substance are related by

$$
n=\frac{m}{M}.
$$

For a solution,

$$
n=cV,
$$

where $V$ must be in dm$^3$ if concentration is in mol dm$^{-3}$. A volume of 250 cm$^3$ is 0.250 dm$^3$, not 250 dm$^3$.

## A limiting reagent example

Consider

$$
2\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{O_2}\rightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

Suppose there are 3.0 mol of $\mathrm{H_2}$ and 2.0 mol of $\mathrm{O_2}$. The available hydrogen would require 1.5 mol of oxygen. Since 2.0 mol are available, hydrogen is limiting.

The mole ratio between $\mathrm{H_2}$ and water is 1:1, so 3.0 mol of water can form. Identifying the limiting reagent before calculating product prevents an impossible answer.

## Yield and atom economy

Percentage yield compares actual product to the theoretical maximum:

$$
\%\ \mathrm{yield}=\frac{\mathrm{actual\ yield}}{\mathrm{theoretical\ yield}}\times100.
$$

It is not a correction factor to apply before working out the limiting reactant. First find the theoretical amount from the balanced equation, then compare it with what was obtained.

## Errors worth checking

- Coefficients change mole ratios, never subscripts in a formula.
- Relative formula mass has units of g mol$^{-1}$ when used as molar mass.
- State which reactant is limiting and show the comparison.
- Round only at the end unless a question specifies otherwise.
