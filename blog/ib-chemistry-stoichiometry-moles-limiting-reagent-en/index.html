IB Chemistry: stoichiometry, moles and limiting reagent problems

IB Chemistry stoichiometry explained through balanced equations, moles, concentrations, gases and limiting reagent calculations.

Stoichiometry is the link between a balanced equation and measurable amounts. The coefficients describe mole ratios; they do not describe masses or volumes until those quantities have been converted to moles.

Direct answer. Balance the equation first, convert every known amount to moles, apply the coefficient ratio, then convert only the requested amount back to mass, volume, concentration or particle number.

The mole as a bridge

Mass and amount of substance are related by

n=mM.n=\frac{m}{M}.n=Mm​.

For a solution,

n=cV,n=cV,n=cV,

where VVV must be in dm3^33 if concentration is in mol dm−3^{-3}−3. A volume of 250 cm3^33 is 0.250 dm3^33, not 250 dm3^33.

A limiting reagent example

Consider

2H2+O2→2H2O.2\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{O_2}\rightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}.2H2​+O2​→2H2​O.

Suppose there are 3.0 mol of H2\mathrm{H_2}H2​ and 2.0 mol of O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​. The available hydrogen would require 1.5 mol of oxygen. Since 2.0 mol are available, hydrogen is limiting.

The mole ratio between H2\mathrm{H_2}H2​ and water is 1:1, so 3.0 mol of water can form. Identifying the limiting reagent before calculating product prevents an impossible answer.

Yield and atom economy

Percentage yield compares actual product to the theoretical maximum:

% yield=actual yieldtheoretical yield×100.\%\ \mathrm{yield}=\frac{\mathrm{actual\ yield}}{\mathrm{theoretical\ yield}}\times100.% yield=theoretical yieldactual yield​×100.

It is not a correction factor to apply before working out the limiting reactant. First find the theoretical amount from the balanced equation, then compare it with what was obtained.

The calculation has two distinct conversions: volume to dm3^33, then moles to mass. Combining them in one unexplained calculator line makes mistakes difficult to find.

Experimental quantities are not exact

Measured masses, volumes and concentrations carry uncertainty. Keep guard digits during calculation, report an appropriate final precision and distinguish an experimental yield below 100% from an arithmetic error.

A gas-volume route through a calculation

If a reaction question gives a gas volume, the same route still applies: volume becomes amount, the balanced-equation ratio is used, and the requested quantity is recovered at the end. Do not apply a molar gas volume before checking the stated temperature and pressure. Those conditions decide which relationship is valid.

For example, if a stated molar volume is 24.0 dm3 mol−124.0\ \mathrm{dm^3\,mol^{-1}}24.0 dm3mol−1, then 0.480 dm30.480\ \mathrm{dm^3}0.480 dm3 represents 0.0200 mol0.0200\ \mathrm{mol}0.0200 mol. That conversion is a starting point, not evidence that any other species also has 0.0200 mol0.0200\ \mathrm{mol}0.0200 mol: the coefficients decide that next step.

Errors worth checking

  • Coefficients change mole ratios, never subscripts in a formula.
  • Relative formula mass has units of g mol−1^{-1}−1 when used as molar mass.
  • State which reactant is limiting and show the comparison.
  • Round only at the end unless a question specifies otherwise.
