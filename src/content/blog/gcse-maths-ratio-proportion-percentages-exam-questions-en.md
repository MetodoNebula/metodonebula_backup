Ratio, proportion and percentage questions reward structure more than speed. A GCSE exam can hide the same calculation inside a recipe, a scale drawing, a sale price or a population model. The reliable first move is to decide whether the quantities change by a fixed amount, a fixed multiplier or a stated ratio.

> **Direct answer.** Use a multiplier for percentage change, a unitary method or scale factor for direct proportion, and check whether the total number of ratio parts matches the quantity you are splitting.

## Ratio: split the total into parts first

Divide £420 in the ratio $3:4$. The total number of parts is:

$$
3+4=7.
$$

One part is $420\div 7=60$. The two shares are therefore:

$$
3\times60=180,\qquad 4\times60=240.
$$

Always add the final shares back together. It catches a surprising number of calculator and transcription mistakes.

## Direct and inverse proportion

When $y$ is directly proportional to $x$:

$$
y=kx.
$$

If $y=18$ when $x=6$, then $k=3$, so $y=3x$. A doubling of $x$ doubles $y$.

For inverse proportion:

$$
y=\frac{k}{x}.
$$

If six workers take ten hours at the same rate, twelve workers take five hours. The product of the paired values stays constant; it is not a direct-proportion situation just because both numbers appear in the question.

## Percentage increase and decrease: work with multipliers

A 15% increase means multiply by:

$$
1+0.15=1.15.
$$

A 15% decrease means multiply by $0.85$. For compound change, multiply repeatedly. An £800 value that rises by 4% for three years is:

$$
800\times1.04^3.
$$

Do not add $4+4+4$ to the original percentage. Each year's change applies to the value after the previous year.

## Reverse percentages: divide by the multiplier

A coat costs £69 after a 20% reduction. The sale price represents 80% of the original, so:

$$
\text{original price}=\frac{69}{0.80}=86.25.
$$

Multiplying £69 by $1.20$ would be wrong: it treats the reduced price as though it were the original value.

## Exam-style question: percentage change after a discount

A phone is reduced by 25% and then increased by 25%. Is it back at its original price?

Start with £100. After the reduction:

$$
100\times0.75=75.
$$

After the increase:

$$
75\times1.25=93.75.
$$

The final price is 6.25% below the original. Equal percentage rises and falls do not cancel because they act on different starting values.

## A short checklist before you commit an answer

1. Have you identified the original amount?
2. Is this a ratio, direct proportion, inverse proportion or percentage multiplier question?
3. Are the units compatible?
4. Does an increase give a sensible larger result?
5. For a reverse percentage, have you divided by the multiplier?
