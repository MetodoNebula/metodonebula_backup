IB Chemistry acids, bases, pH and titration calculations

Acid–base questions become manageable when three ideas remain separate: the reaction equation, the amount of substance and the logarithmic pH scale. Mixing them too early is the usual reason a correct-looking calculation produces the wrong answer.

Direct answer. Start from the relevant equation, convert titre and concentration into moles, use the stoichiometric ratio at equivalence, and only then calculate the requested concentration or pH. State whether the acid or base is strong before using a shortcut involving complete dissociation.

Brønsted–Lowry pairs in an equation

An acid donates a proton and a base accepts one. In

HA+H2O⇌A−+H3O+,\mathrm{HA}+\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{A^-}+\mathrm{H_3O^+},HA+H2​O⇌A−+H3​O+,

HA\mathrm{HA}HA and A−\mathrm{A^-}A− are a conjugate pair: losing one proton turns the acid into its conjugate base. Water acts as a base in this equation because it accepts the proton. The labels come from the direction being considered, not from a substance having one permanent role in every reaction.

pH is logarithmic

For an aqueous solution,

pH=−log⁡10[H3O+].\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}[\mathrm{H_3O^+}].pH=−log10​[H3​O+].

If [H3O+]=2.5×10−3 mol dm−3[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]=2.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}[H3​O+]=2.5×10−3 moldm−3, then pH=2.60\mathrm{pH}=2.60pH=2.60. A change of one pH unit is a factor of ten in hydronium-ion concentration, not an additive change in acidity.

For a strong monoprotic acid at an appropriate concentration, the acid concentration can be used as [H3O+][\mathrm{H_3O^+}][H3​O+] because dissociation is effectively complete. That shortcut does not apply automatically to a weak acid; equilibrium matters there.

Worked titration: finding an unknown concentration

n(OH−)=0.150×0.01860=2.79×10−3 mol.n(\mathrm{OH^-})=0.150\times0.01860=2.79\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}.n(OH−)=0.150×0.01860=2.79×10−3 mol.

When the coefficients are not 1:1

For sulfuric acid and sodium hydroxide,

H2SO4+2NaOH→Na2SO4+2H2O,\mathrm{H_2SO_4}+2\mathrm{NaOH}\rightarrow\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}+2\mathrm{H_2O},H2​SO4​+2NaOH→Na2​SO4​+2H2​O,

two moles of hydroxide react for each mole of sulfuric acid. If the alkali amount is 0.0200 mol0.0200\ \mathrm{mol}0.0200 mol, the acid amount at equivalence is 0.0100 mol0.0100\ \mathrm{mol}0.0100 mol. Writing the balanced equation before inserting values prevents the common factor-of-two error.

Weak acids and equilibrium language

A weak acid is not simply a dilute acid. Weak refers to incomplete dissociation; dilute refers to low concentration. A concentrated weak acid and a dilute strong acid are both possible. For a weak acid, the equilibrium constant describes the balance between undissociated acid and ions, so the concentration of acid is not identical to [H3O+][\mathrm{H_3O^+}][H3​O+].

Use the equilibrium reasoning from the equilibrium-constant guide when an acid-dissociation calculation requires it: define the species, construct the expression and check whether an approximation is justified.

Titration data need interpretation

Select concordant titres before calculating a mean, and report the volume to the precision allowed by the apparatus. A colour change tells you an indicator endpoint has been reached; it is an experimental estimate of the equivalence point, not a magic proof that every measurement is exact. A rough titre is useful for locating the endpoint but should not normally be included in the final mean.

Checks before committing to an answer

  • Convert every titre from cm3^33 to dm3^33 before using n=cVn=cVn=cV.
  • Use the balanced equation, even if a shortcut seems familiar.
  • Give pH to an appropriate number of decimal places after using the logarithm.
  • Keep strength, concentration and amount as different ideas.
  • Ask whether the question concerns an equivalence point, an endpoint or an equilibrium mixture.
